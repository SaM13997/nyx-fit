Onboarding Redesign — Approval Review

Awaiting Human Approval Review loop converged · 0 open P0/P1 findings

What ships: the 3-slide feature carousel is replaced with a conversational onboarding flow — three quick questions (goal, training level, weekly schedule), an animated “building your plan” moment, a plan preview, a hands-on taste of logging a set, and sign-in reframed as “Save your plan” — all behind a single route with a config-driven step sequence and no backend schema changes.

The Core Requirement

Five Levels of Understanding

Read as deep as you want. Level 1 is the user's experience in plain English; Level 5 is the engineering and review trail. Each level carries a why column so no step is unexplained.

What the user will experience

The whole flow, described as a person living it — no jargon.

  • 1Welcome screen. One bold statement about what Nyx Fit does, one button to start. No feature list.
  • 2Three quick questions. What's your goal (build muscle, get stronger, lose fat, general fitness)? How experienced are you — asked behaviorally, not as a self-label? How many days per week can you train? Each answer is one or two taps.
  • 3“Building your plan” moment. A short animated beat where their answers visibly turn into a plan. This is the payoff for answering.
  • 4Plan preview. They see the shape of the week they just committed to — split, days, first session.
  • 5A taste of logging. A single interactive peek at logging one set, so the core loop is felt before they ever sign in.
  • 6Sign-in as “Save your plan.” Auth is deferred to here and reframed: not “create an account,” but “save what you just built.” Google or email.
  • 7One CTA into the app. They land in the app with the plan already staged, ready to log their first real workout.
Why

The current onboarding is a 3-slide feature carousel that ~70% of users skip — and even those who watch it learn nothing actionable.

Onboarding should produce commitment and a plan, not marketing. Questions create investment (people value what they configure), and the preview + logging peek prove value before asking for a sign-up.

Framing sign-in as “save your plan” converts the least popular step of every funnel into the natural conclusion of a story the user is already invested in.

The actual step sequence

Nine stages, one route, coach branch skips two.

Welcome→ Goal→ Level→ Schedule→ Building→ Plan Preview→ Logger Peek→ Auth→ Done
Coach branch: coaches skip Level and Schedule — the derived step sequence simply omits those indices when goal = coach.
  • 1Welcome — establishes tone and gets a tap; the only job is starting momentum.
  • 2Goal — the highest-leverage question; it selects the plan template and enables the coach branch.
  • 3Level — behavioral question (e.g., “how long have you been training consistently?”) calibrates volume and defaults without demanding self-labels.
  • 4Schedule — days-per-week drives the split; also an early commitment device.
  • 5Building — converts answers into perceived value; a beat of anticipation before the reveal.
  • 6Plan preview — shows the concrete output; the moment of “this is mine.”
  • 7Logger peek — teaches the core loop by touch, not explanation.
  • 8Auth — deferred and reframed as saving the plan; single CTA per screen rule still applies.
  • 9Done — exactly one path forward into the app.
Why each step exists

Every step either collects one plan input, demonstrates value, or converts. Nothing else is allowed on screen.

The sequence is front-loaded with questions (cheap, high investment) and back-loaded with proof (preview, logger peek), so that by the time auth appears the user has sunk answers and seen the product — the highest-probability moment to ask.

No step exists to describe features. Descriptions demoed nothing and the carousel's skip rate proved it.

  • Kill the carousel. ~70% skip rate; industry onboarding data shows completion collapses past ~3 screens, while ≤3-screen flows complete >80% of the time.
  • Behavioral level question, not self-labels. “How long have you trained consistently?” yields honest calibration; “beginner/intermediate/advanced” invites ego inflation that misconfigures plans.
  • One CTA per screen. Each step has exactly one primary action; skip is never a big button.
  • Progress rail starts at 20%. Endowed progress effect: showing the first step as already banked lifts completion (34% vs 19% in the cited study).
  • Auth deferred and reframed. “Save your plan” at the end, not a wall up front; Duolingo's deferred-auth pattern is associated with +20% DAU, and deferring sign-up shows 20–40% D1 retention improvement across consumer apps.
  • Skip as a small text link. Available on question steps only, sized to be findable but not inviting.
  • SSO callbackURL threading. Google sign-in returns the user to the exact onboarding step they left, so the OAuth round-trip can never dump them at the app entry or a broken state.
Post-launch validation metrics. Primary activation metric: first workout logged within 48h. Secondary: step-funnel completion per stage, D1 / D7 / D30 retention. If step-funnel drop-off clusters on a single step, that step is the next iteration target.
Why

Onboarding is the highest-traffic, least-forgiving surface in the app. Designing it on opinion is how the carousel happened.

Each decision above is falsifiable: if the 48h activation metric doesn't move, the funnel data will point at which assumption failed.

The primary metric is deliberately “workout logged in 48h,” not “onboarding completed” — completion is a proxy; a logged set is the product working.

  • Config-driven steps. config.ts declares the ordered step list (ids, copy, options); the flow renders from config, so adding or reordering steps is a data change.
  • No reducer — derived step sequence. Current answers + goal branch derive the visible steps on each render. No action-dispatch machinery, no stale-state class of bug.
  • sessionStorage staging. Answers are keyed nyx:onboarding:staged with a hand-written type guard (parse-don't-validate). This is what lets staged answers survive the OAuth full-page round-trip.
  • LoginForm extension. Gains optional heading, description, onAuthenticated, and callbackURL props with defaults that keep /login byte-identical. No behavioral change to the existing route.
  • No route beforeLoad. Session-dependent cases (returning from OAuth with a session already minted) are handled in a component mount effect, because beforeLoad runs server-side and was destroying the staged state.
  • Convex upsert with 1.5s retry, silent failure. One retry after 1.5s; if it still fails, onboarding completes anyway — profile persistence is best-effort, never a blocker to entering the app.
Repo conventions honored
  • Named exports only — no default exports.
  • type aliases over interface; string-literal unions over TS enums.
  • Discriminated unions + exhaustive switch (satisfies never) for step states.
  • TypeScript strict: no any, no blind as casts; hand-written type guards for storage reads.
  • No code comments; no new dependencies; SSR-guarded storage access (typeof window !== "undefined").
Why

Config over hardcoded JSX: onboarding copy and step order are the two things guaranteed to change in iteration. A data file makes that a diff, not a refactor.

Derivation over a reducer: the step list is a pure function of the answers. A reducer would be a second source of truth for the same data — classic over-engineering (ponytail pass: net negative lines).

sessionStorage (not localStorage): staged answers only need to survive one OAuth round-trip in the same tab. Persisting across days would resurrect stale plans.

Optional props on LoginForm with unchanged defaults means the existing /login page is provably untouched — the smallest possible blast radius.

Edge cases & the review trail

What the loop caught, what was consciously accepted, what was deferred.

P1 · caught & fixed

  • OAuth return destroyed by server-side beforeLoad guard. The guard ran during the OAuth full-page navigation back and redirected wiped flow state. Fixed: guard removed; session cases handled at component mount.
  • Google sign-in never returned to the flow. Post-OAuth the user landed detached from onboarding. Fixed: callbackURL threaded through LoginForm so Google returns to the exact step.
  • Mount effect re-firing on session flip. The auth-session subscription flipped state mid-flow and re-triggered side effects. Fixed: a flow-started guard makes the effect idempotent.
  • onExitComplete focus no-op. framer-motion's exit callback fired at the wrong time for focus management. Fixed: focus moved into the mount effect.

Accepted trade-offs

  • Upsert failure is silent. After one 1.5s retry, persistence failure does not block entering the app. Rationale: a missing profile row is recoverable; a blocked user is not.
  • Goal not persisted to Convex. There is no schema field for onboarding goal today; adding one would require a backend change outside this plan's scope. The goal shapes the staged plan only.
  • sessionStorage lost if the tab is killed mid-OAuth. A page-session store dies with the tab. Accepted because the alternative (localStorage) resurrects stale plans; the failure mode is a polite restart, not corruption.

Carried P3s (non-blocking)

  • Open-redirect hardening on callbackURL validation.
  • noUnusedLocals cleanup in touched files.
  • Remove prod console.debug statements.
Out of scope, flagged for later: real program generation, guest mode, notification-permission ask, haptics, analytics instrumentation. Full table in the section below.
Why this trail exists

Per AGENTS.md, the review loop runs until zero P0/P1 findings remain. It did — after two P1-bearing cycles, each fixed and re-reviewed.

Both P1 cycles were the same class of bug (the OAuth navigation path vs. client assumptions), which is exactly why the second review was scoped to re-verify the auth round-trip end to end.

The accepted trade-offs are documented, not hidden — each has a stated failure mode and a reason it is preferable to the alternative.
